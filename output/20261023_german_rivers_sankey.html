How to read the flows & reference data

For each reach, we take the river’s downstream reference mean and subtract the reference flows of illustrated tributaries that join later. Unrepresented headwaters and catchment contributions are lumped at the first illustrated city. The allocation makes the ribbons grow at displayed confluences without adding a tributary twice. It does not locate actual runoff or measure local discharge; even an unchanged reference carried along a river is not a city-gauge observation.

The Weser’s unrepresented catchment contribution is also lumped at its first stop, Hann. Münden; its width there is not the measured sum of Fulda and Werra. The first illustrated city is not a river’s source. Published gauge means and estimates span different periods, so this is an explanatory network, not a calibrated or common-period water balance. For freshwater, 1 m³/s is approximately 1 tonne of water per second.

Most reference values come from Wikipedia’s German-river compilation, supplemented by individual river references and a BAW report for Leda. The Leda reference includes Jümme freshwater input, excluding tidal exchange. BfG explains mean discharge (MQ). References checked 27 October 2026.

Confluence locations & geographical simplifications

Most nodes are cities; smaller towns and districts preserve important junctions. Confluences close to a city are grouped with it where described below. The Havel meets the Elbe at Gnevsdorf, downstream of Havelberg; Berlin-Mitte and Spandau, and Regensburg-Mariaort and Regensburg, are separate stops.

Routes continue outside Germany. The Rhine delta, the Oder’s route via the Szczecin Lagoon, and the Donau’s route from Passau to the Black Sea are collapsed. Additional foreign discharge, most tributaries, delta branches, canals and transfers are not modelled. Named ribbon routes indicate the river being followed; neither link length nor node spacing represents distance.
