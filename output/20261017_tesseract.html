What am I looking at?

A tesseract is the four-dimensional counterpart of a cube. Its boundary has eight cubic cells, 24 square faces, 32 edges, and 16 vertices. Here it rotates in the x–w plane before being projected into three dimensions. The apparent folding is caused by that projection: the original shape remains rigid in four dimensions.

At 0°, two cubes appear nested and their corresponding corners are joined. Dragging changes your 3D viewpoint; the rotation slider changes the 4D orientation. This is a projection of the whole tesseract, rather than a slice through it.
